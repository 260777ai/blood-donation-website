Why your donation matters

Hospitals need a steady supply of blood every single day, for surgeries, accidents, childbirth complications, cancer treatment and blood disorders.

  • One donation, several patients

    A single unit is separated into red cells, plasma and platelets, so it can help more than one person. It is commonly said that one donation can help save up to three lives.

  • Blood has a short shelf life

    Red cells last about 42 days and platelets only a few days. That is why regular donors are needed, not just a one-time rush after a disaster.

  • It is quick and safe

    The donation itself takes around 10 to 15 minutes. New, sterile, single-use equipment is used for every donor.

What happens on donation day

The whole visit usually takes about an hour.

  1. Registration

    You fill in a short form and show an ID proof.

  2. Health check

    Staff check your weight, pulse, blood pressure and haemoglobin, and ask a few health questions.

  3. Donation

    You sit or lie down while about 350 to 450 ml of blood is collected in 10 to 15 minutes.

  4. Rest and refresh

    You rest for a few minutes and have a snack and a drink before leaving.

Before and after

Before you donate

  • Sleep well the night before.
  • Eat a normal meal and drink extra water.
  • Carry a photo ID.

After you donate

  • Drink more fluids for the next day.
  • Avoid heavy exercise and lifting for 24 hours.
  • Sit down and tell someone if you feel dizzy.

Common questions

Does donating blood hurt?

You feel a quick pinch when the needle goes in. After that most people feel little or nothing.

Will I feel weak afterwards?

Your body replaces the fluid within about a day. Resting, eating well and drinking water helps. Most people carry on with normal activities the same day.

How often can I donate?

Whole blood can generally be donated every 3 months. Your blood bank will confirm the gap that applies to you.

Can I get any disease from donating?

No. Blood banks use a new, sterile, single-use needle and bag for every donor.
